The costume

Whoever opens the door should be able to tell in one second that you are friendly and that you work for us. Everything below follows from that.

Good bets

  • Anything a kid would smile at: an animal, a chef, an astronaut, a farmer, a superhero
  • Something purple, which does double duty as a costume and a cue
  • A costume you can explain in three words
  • Your staff badge visible over the top of all of it

Leave at home

  • Full face masks and heavy makeup. Folks need to recognize a person.
  • Blood, gore, wounds, or anything with a weapon, real or foam
  • Anything political, or any elected official
  • Anything that plays a condition or a patient for laughs

The test: would you wear it trick or treating with a five year old? Then it works. Would you wear it to a haunted house? Then it does not.

The pumpkin

Purple is the whole point, so lead with the color and let the decoration be simple.

If you are painting

  1. Two thin coats of purple beat one thick coat. Acrylic on a craft pumpkin will streak if you load the brush.
  2. Give each coat about twenty minutes. Be sure to paint the night before, not the morning of.
  3. Leave the stem unpainted. It reads as a pumpkin faster that way.
  4. Add one thing and stop. A name, a simple ribbon, our logo. Three elements is where it starts looking cluttered.

If you want the logo on it, pull an official file from the Hub and print it as a stencil or a sticker rather than freehanding it. Concern & Awareness, then the Logo Library.

If you are carving

  • Paint the outside purple first and let it dry, then carve. Carving first means painting around a hole.
  • Simple, wide cuts. Thin detail collapses in a day and you are handing this to someone.
  • A real pumpkin starts going within a few days of carving. Carve it the day before the drop by, not the week before.
  • Battery tea light, not a candle. No office is going to let you leave an open flame.

The drop by

Short visit, warm tone, one thing left behind. You are not asking for a vote at the door.

  1. Call ahead. Reach the staff member you already know. Say you are stopping by with something seasonal and ask what time works.
  2. Keep it to five minutes. Hand over the pumpkin, say thank you for something specific, and go. Length is not the point here.
  3. Leave one piece of paper. The pumpkin is the reason they will remember the visit. The paper is the reason it matters later.
  4. Take a photo before you go in, costume and pumpkin together. Ask the office before you photograph anyone on staff.
  5. Log it in Quorum the same day, while you still remember who you talked to.
